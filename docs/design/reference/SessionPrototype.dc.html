Why leap years exist

Earth takes about 365.2422 days to circle the Sun, not a neat 365. A 365-day calendar slips behind the seasons by almost six hours a year.

Julius Caesar added a leap day every fourth year in 46 BC. That assumes 365.25 days, about eleven minutes too long.

The Gregorian calendar of 1582 fixed it. Century years skip the leap day unless they divide evenly by 400.
